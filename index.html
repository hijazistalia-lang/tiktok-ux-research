01 · Evaluative product research

StarScore

User feedbackMazeTypeformUsability analysisFigma

Transforming an indistinct interface into a clearer, more engaging journey.

The starting UI felt bland and unexciting. Customers did not consistently understand what different tabs did or how one feature differed from another. My work connected the reported confusion to interface hierarchy, navigation, labeling, and feature presentation—then used those insights to guide the redesign.

Open complete StarScore Figma project ↗
01 / FRAME

Research question

Why were customers struggling to understand and distinguish the product’s features?

02 / LISTEN

Collect feedback

Reviewed customer responses and usability signals about tabs, visual interest, and the end-to-end journey.

03 / SYNTHESIZE

Identify patterns

Grouped recurring confusion around feature purpose, hierarchy, differentiation, and unclear paths.

04 / DESIGN

Translate findings

Reworked navigation, tab distinction, interface hierarchy, and feature presentation in Figma.

05 / EVALUATE

Measure response

Compared reported customer satisfaction before and after the redesign.

+28percentage-point increase
Before redesign56%
After redesign84%

Based on the project’s before-and-after customer-satisfaction feedback. The participant count will be added when the original study record is recovered.

Finding

The issue was not only visual appeal. Customers lacked a reliable mental model for what each tab and feature was for.

Design response

Stronger hierarchy, recognizable feature identities, and clearer paths made the interface easier to interpret and more exciting to explore.

Ultimate outcome

Reported satisfaction increased from 56% to 84%, while feedback highlighted clearer feature distinction and a more understandable journey.

02 · Foundational market research

Kairos

Survey designInterviewsSegmentationMarket analysisProduct strategy

Using audience research to narrow an ambitious product vision.

Kairos was simultaneously exploring a professional chart tool, interactive horoscope, personal reference notebook, and future social platform. I helped turn a broad product question—“What should this become?”—into research about audience sophistication, behavior, unmet needs, feature value, and market positioning.

Open complete Kairos Figma project ↗
01 / DEFINE

Business problem

The app was trying to serve too many purposes without a clearly prioritized audience.

02 / RECRUIT

Two channels

Reached existing subscribers and broader Reddit/Discord communities to compare different audiences.

03 / MEASURE

Survey behavior

Explored tenure, frequency, systems used, desired features, frustrations, and social engagement.

04 / COMPARE

Segment needs

Contrasted advanced users’ professional workflows with more casual consumer preferences.

05 / RECOMMEND

Guide direction

Converted the patterns into feature, positioning, usability, and acquisition recommendations.

Ultimate finding and product recommendation

The strongest initial position was not a generic horoscope app. The research supported focusing Kairos on experienced users through professional tools, interactive charts, stronger organization, and clearer navigation. Social publishing and broad horoscope content remained valuable hypotheses, but they should follow the core professional experience rather than compete with it. Instagram also emerged as the strongest shared acquisition channel. Because the sample was small and channel-dependent, I recommended larger validation before an irreversible roadmap decision.

Limitations: n=19 total; different recruitment channels; likely subscriber bias; some question bases varied because respondents skipped items. Percentages describe these samples and are not population estimates.
