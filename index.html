Making features understandable—and the experience worth exploring.

The original interface felt visually flat, and users struggled to understand the purpose of different tabs or distinguish one feature from another. I researched the friction, reorganized the journey, clarified feature relationships, and redesigned the visual system.

Case study 02 · Foundational research

Kairos

Survey designUser interviewsAudience segmentationMarket insightProduct strategy

Narrowing an ambitious app by learning what distinct audiences actually value.

Kairos was evolving into a professional astrology tool, interactive horoscope, reference notebook, and possible social platform. The product risk was clear: it was trying to do too much. I designed research to identify the audience, uncover unmet needs, and guide feature prioritization.

Two recruitment channels, one product-direction question

Would Kairos create more value as an advanced professional tool or as a broader consumer astrology experience?

From findings to product direction

The evidence supported prioritizing advanced users with professional tools, interactive charts, improved organization, and clearer navigation—while treating social features and mass-market horoscope content as secondary hypotheses. Instagram emerged as a promising channel for audience growth. I also documented the risk of sample bias and recommended larger-scale validation before making irreversible roadmap decisions.

Research limitation: the total sample was small (n=19), recruitment channels differed, and the subscriber sample likely overrepresented experienced Kairos users. Percentages describe these respondents and are not presented as population estimates.
